<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A week or so ago, <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/safety.bsky.app/post/3mukm26duos2c">BlueSky posted</a> about penalizing dogpiling and harassment on the platform. And while I think that’s absolutely a great idea, I don’t love the fact that it seems to be focused on after-the-fact enforcement. The BlueSky/Atmosphere philosophy seems like it should be focused on user control of their experience, managing their use of specific safety mechanisms. </p>




<p class="wp-block-paragraph">So, in the spirit of “making things is the best way to complain”, I built a very rough “BlueSky circuit breaker”:  <a href="https://github.com/ajdecon/bsky-circuit-breaker">https://github.com/ajdecon/bsky-circuit-breaker</a> </p>




<span id="more-494"></span>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>This is <em>not at all production-ready</em></strong><em>. </em>I have a day job and it’s not maintaining mod infrastructure. And I know from colleagues how much work that really is. This is sincerely just a proof-of-concept in the true sense: throw-away code to show a given idea may work.</p>




<p class="wp-block-paragraph">This tiny tool demonstrates a <em>proof of concept</em> for:</p>




<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Locking a post’s interactions when too many people interact with it</li>



<li>Locking it when too many people interact at a given rate (interactions/hour)</li>



<li>Locking a post when a person with tons of followers quote-post it</li>



<li>Locking a post if you’re getting “ratio’d” with too many replies vs likes</li>
</ul>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Experimentally, the tool also includes an option to call out to a local LLM to do sentiment analysis on replies. Things like “does this contain slurs”, or “is it weirdly hostile to the original post author”, or “is the reply completely off-topic”. This is really r<em>eally </em>imperfect, but potentially useful to folks like me who are small-ish posters with day jobs who don’t want to be caught in an accidental reply storm. </p>




<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The script itself is a one-and-done affair: it runs, analyzes your feed, takes action if you allow it, and exits. On initial development I envisioned running this in a cron-like setup. I will probably run this, or something like it, on a once-an-hour schedule on a personal VM.</p>




<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A production-like service should probably be more robust, but like I said: this is a proof-of-concept.</p>




<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In any case, this is what I’d like to see social media moderation look like: tools to control interactions from others, including “oh God” circuit breakers for when you get way more attention than you expected. </p>




<p class="wp-block-paragraph">IMHO, this kind of circuit breaker should be available be default! Most social media accounts are small, after all. While I recognize the challenges of responding instantly to dogpiles — distributed systems are hard — a cron-like function that scans accounts and performs post locks based on user config should not be too onerous.</p>




<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Anyway, in the spirit of open source: here’s the code! Do what you like.</p>




<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Disclaimer: the design and much of the initial code of this script was me, directly. I then enlisted the help of qwen3-coder:30b for minor code fixes, in particular atproto API bits. What can I say, I was more interested in the logic than the external APIs.</p>




<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Regardless, the code is MIT-licensed. I’m not sure it actually is good for much <em>directly</em>, but it might be inspiring for someone who wants to build circuit breakers into real apps. We can only hope.</p>
